Module 05 · Empirical research

Factor exposures, tested against frozen data

Daily ETF returns are regressed on the Fama–French five factors plus momentum, with HAC confidence intervals, rolling estimates, a chronological holdout, and corrections for testing six alphas at once. The result is an auditable estimate—not a stylized profile.

Revised 3 August 2026 · research record

Decision summary

The labels were informative; the apparent alpha was not robust

Bottom line

The advertised factor exposures were recovered, but no ETF alpha survived a 5% false-discovery threshold after six simultaneous tests.

Decision affected

Evaluate a style fund by measured exposures, uncertainty, stability, and implementation—not by branding or one unadjusted alpha result.

Who should care

Fund selectors, portfolio analysts, wealth and asset-management teams, quantitative researchers, and model reviewers.

Caveat

The conclusion is conditional on six ETFs, the selected factor menu, one 2015–2026 vintage, and one chronological split. Exposure estimates can change across regimes and specifications.

Estimate 01

ETF exposures with 95% confidence intervals

For each ETF, the model is a time-series regression of daily excess returns on MKT–RF, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA, and momentum. Intervals use Newey–West HAC covariance estimates with five lags. This is an exposure study—not the two-pass Fama–MacBeth procedure used to estimate cross-sectional factor premia.1

Estimate 02

Rolling versus full-sample QQQ betas

A full-sample beta compresses eleven years into one number. The lines below re-estimate QQQ on a trailing 252-observation window; dashed references show the corresponding full-sample estimates. Instability is evidence about model scope, not chart noise.

Estimate 03

Out-of-sample explanatory decay

The sample is split in half chronologically. Coefficients estimated on the first half are frozen, then applied to the second. Predictive R² is benchmarked against the holdout mean. A lower bar in the holdout is the measured cost of transporting the exposure map through time.

Multiplicity audit

What survives six simultaneous alpha tests?

Raw significance is not a research conclusion. The table reports unadjusted p-values, Holm family-wise adjusted p-values, and Benjamini–Hochberg q-values. On this snapshot, no ETF alpha survives a 5% false-discovery threshold—an important correction to the tempting standalone QQQ result.2
