Quantitative models and diagnostics

Interactive models for pricing, portfolio construction, risk, factor attribution, and simulation

Model suite

Five implementations for assumption sensitivity and diagnostic analysis

InteractiveNumerical model

Option valuation under model and parameter variation

The option-pricing model runs Black–Scholes–Merton beside a Cox–Ross–Rubinstein tree. Change spot, strike, maturity, rate, dividend yield, or volatility. Prices and Greeks move with the change.

Use it to
Inspect no-arbitrage pricing, sensitivity, and numerical convergence
Do not use it to
Represent every exercise feature or observed market dynamic
Open the pricing model →
InteractiveIllustrative model

Portfolio geometry under uncertain inputs

The efficient-frontier model samples twenty thousand long-only portfolios across six illustrative asset classes. It then compares minimum-variance, maximum-Sharpe, and equal-weight answers.

Use it to
Inspect risk–return geometry, constraints, and assumption sensitivity
Do not use it to
Treat illustrative moments as forecasts or recommendations
Analyze allocation sensitivity →
EmpiricalExecuted study

Historical VaR under empirical backtesting

The market-risk study builds a cost-aware, leak-free portfolio path. It then runs block-bootstrap intervals, formal VaR coverage tests, and dated stress windows.

Use it to
Inspect risk-model validation, calibration, and stress design
Do not use it to
Treat one frozen history as a bound on future losses
Inspect the VaR backtest →
EmpiricalExecuted study

Factor exposure estimation and inference

The factor study runs six-factor regressions on ETF returns. It adds HAC intervals, rolling exposure, chronological holdouts, and false-discovery corrections.

Use it to
Inspect attribution, product-label verification, and statistical governance
Do not use it to
Interpret alpha independently of the selected factor model
Review factor estimates →
InteractiveScenario model

Stochastic-process behavior and scenario capacity

The stochastic-process model simulates GBM, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck, Merton jumps, Heston stochastic volatility, and regime switching. A seeded generator makes each run repeatable.

Use it to
Inspect path behavior, distribution shape, and model differences
Do not use it to
Treat a simulated process as a market forecast
Run stochastic simulations →
